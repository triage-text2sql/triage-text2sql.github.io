Targeting the Missing Supervision: From Structural Coverage Gaps to Failure-Aware Inference for Text-to-SQL

Paper ID: 1642
Under submission to VLDB 2027.

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have substantially advanced Text-to-SQL, yet they remain brittle on complex (enterprise) schemas, where errors often arise from incorrect join paths, ambiguous columns, and coupled relational operations. We identify a fundamental bottleneck behind these failures: supervision can be plentiful while leaving important regions of a target schema structurally under-covered. We address this bottleneck with a unified approach that closes the loop from missing structural supervision to failure-aware inference. First, we formulate challenging-data synthesis as structural coverage expansion over a database's foreign-key graph, we call it SPLICE. It explicitly identifies under-covered schema paths to generate grounded (NLQ, SQL) pairs. We then introduce TRIAGE, which mines residual errors of a frontier LLM model (Claude Opus 4.7) to derive an empirical failure taxonomy, instantiates failure-specific specialists, and reconciles their complementary candidates using execution feedback; a calibrated LLM-as-a-judge enables ground-truth-SQL-free validation and targeted refinement of SQL.

We conduct an extensive empirical evaluation across established Text-to-SQL benchmarks. On BIRD data, only 750 SPLICE-generated examples achieve 73.2% execution accuracy when used as few-shot exemplars with Claude Opus 4.7, outperforming a size-matched subset of the 2.5M-example SynSQL-2.5M corpus (72.6%). The complete TRIAGE with Claude Opus 4.8 reaches 78.6% execution accuracy on the BIRD development set under oracle knowledge and perfect schema information, exceeding the reported BIRD leaderboard result of 77.77% achieved by SiriusAI-SQL. On Spider 2.0-SQLite data, TRIAGE with Claude Opus 4.8 achieves 52.6% execution accuracy, outperforming most baselines. These results provide evidence that where supervision is missing can matter more than merely increasing synthetic-data volume and that combining structural coverage expansion with failure-aware agentic specialization offers a complementary route to robust Text-to-SQL.
